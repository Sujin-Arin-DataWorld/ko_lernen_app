# Palette-preserving brush study

User's latest clarification: warmth means an illustrator's care, coherent material identities, repeated intentional brush decisions. It does not mean warmer hues. The R3 hue glaze is withdrawn.

## Council synthesis

Three independent voices (pragmatist, critic, skeptic) agreed: fewer fine repeated grains, broader interrupted marks, quiet intervals, material-specific direction. Work on the real native model; keep architecture, palette, camera and lights. Compare a mixed-material facade and the full view at app size.

Strongest dissent/risk: adding brush masks can turn into procedural stains. A change visible only enlarged is insufficient. Painted light/contact shadows would double the scene lighting. Consequently the new atlas is a neutral pigment-load signal only, with mean-centered contrast; existing hue is retained and fine texture contribution is reduced instead of accumulating new dense noise.

## Concrete study

- Input: verified R3 fix checkpoint, preserving the two reported fixes.
- New grayscale atlas has four rhythms: timber, curved clay, broken stone planes, quiet plaster. Shader converts it to luminance, so the small color contamination from generation cannot tint the building.
- Wood/lattice fine painted variation reduced 42/45 percent; stone/tile 22 percent; plaster 18 percent; paper 10 percent. Existing source material identity remains.
- New longitudinal brush masks follow existing timber UVs. Other materials keep source mapping and stable per-island phases. No mesh/UV edits.
- Fine relief reduced; new pigment tooth remains shallow. No hue/saturation/value grade, backdrop/light changes, global filter, new architecture or props.
- This is a native candidate, not user approval, canonical promotion, GLB transfer or app delivery.
