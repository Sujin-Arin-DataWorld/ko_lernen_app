# KOREAN CURRICULUM 1–6 — Master Spine

Basis: National Institute of Korean Language Korean Standard Curriculum + International Standard Curriculum research, CEFR Companion Volume (2020) as a comparison framework, and Hangul Sori's existing A1–B2 content.
Important: CEFR labels below are reference bands only; they are not claimed as official one-to-one equivalences.

| Korean level | Internal books | Working proficiency lens | Human outcome |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 1A / 1B | beginner / A1 reference | survive, introduce self, handle immediate daily needs |
| 2 | 2A / 2B | elementary / A2 reference | independently manage routine life and relationships |
| 3 | 3A / 3B | intermediate / B1 reference | participate in social, study and workplace life |
| 4 | 4A / 4B | upper-intermediate / B2 reference | discuss complex everyday/professional matters with nuance |
| 5 | 5A / 5B | advanced / C1 reference | reason about abstract, public and professional topics |

## Curriculum dimensions required at every level
Communicative situation; function; vocabulary; grammar; pronunciation/phonology; listening; speaking; reading; writing; online interaction; mediation; pragmatics; culture; strategy; assessment; recycling.

## Level 1
Can greet, identify people/things, order food, shop, use transport, arrange simple plans and ask for immediate help.
Pragmatics: 해요체 as default productive register; recognition of 합니다체 and 반말 begins through context, not forced production.
Modern contexts: café, convenience store, delivery pickup, messaging, subway, photo sharing, streaming preferences.
End state: short predictable exchanges without romanization support.

## Level 2
Can narrate recent events, make plans, compare choices, explain simple reasons, ask favors, handle appointments, travel and basic service problems.
Pragmatics: 부탁/거절 softening, age/status awareness, common address terms, switching between polite and casual speech in controlled contexts.
Modern contexts: online shopping/returns, gym, KTX, booking apps, group chats, content recommendations.

## Level 3
Can sustain conversations, tell stories, explain opinions, solve problems, write practical messages/emails and participate in study/work routines.
Pragmatics: indirectness, hedging, turn-taking, disagreement without unnecessary bluntness, 눈치 as observable interactional behavior rather than stereotype.
Modern contexts: job search, team projects, reviews, apartment search, hobbies, dating, workplace chat, creator/content culture.

## Level 4
Can argue a position with reasons/evidence, understand implied stance, summarize sources and adapt language to social/professional settings.
Pragmatics: stance, irony/light sarcasm recognition, disagreement, complaint/escalation, formal vs conversational professional Korean.
Modern contexts: AI tools, hybrid work, sustainability, cost of living, digital privacy, travel behavior, social media discourse.

## Level 5
Can analyze social issues, compare perspectives, interpret news/commentary, present structured arguments and write professional/academic texts.
Topics: demographic change, housing, labor, climate adaptation, media literacy, platform economies, education, technology ethics.
Language focus: discourse markers, nominalization, evidentiality/stance, dense written syntax, register shifts, summary/synthesis.

## Level 6
Can understand and produce nuanced academic/professional/public discourse, including rhetorical intent, ambiguity and culturally loaded implications.
Tasks: seminar debate, policy brief, critical review, presentation/Q&A, negotiation, synthesis of multiple sources, style transformation.
Language focus: genre control, lexical precision, metaphor/idiom, rhetorical framing, advanced honorific/register choices and editing for audience.

## Unit design rule
Every unit starts with a real-world communicative problem, then maps target functions → language → input → noticing → guided production → freer production → feedback → spaced recycling.
No unit is titled only by a grammar form.

## Official benchmark notes
The international standard curriculum research enumerates 10,635 vocabulary items and 336 grammar items across Levels 1–6, and treats topics/functions/tasks/vocabulary/grammar/skills/pronunciation/text/culture/evaluation as coordinated categories. Those figures are benchmarks for coverage auditing, not a license to copy another publisher's lesson sequence.
