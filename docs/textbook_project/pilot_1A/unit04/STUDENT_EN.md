# Korean 1A · Unit 04
# Ordering food and quantities

## By the end of this unit

In a Korean snack bar or café, you can:

- order a food or drink;
- add a simple quantity;
- ask one short question such as “Is it very spicy?”;
- change one condition, such as mild / iced / to go;
- understand a few common staff-side service phrases.

You do **not** need to reproduce every sentence the staff member uses.

---

## 1. Listen first

### Scene: at a bunsik restaurant

**Staff**  
주문하시겠어요?

**Customer**  
떡볶이 하나 주세요.

**Customer**  
많이 매워요?

**Staff**  
조금 매워요. 안 매운 것도 있어요.

**Customer**  
그럼 안 매운 걸로 주세요.

**Staff**  
네, 안 매운 걸로 드릴게요.

### Check the meaning

1. What does the customer order?
2. Do they want the spicy or mild version?
3. Which sentences are useful for the learner to say? Which ones are mainly useful to understand?

### Your production target

**N 하나 주세요.**  
One N, please.

**많이 매워요?**  
Is it very spicy?

**안 매운 걸로 주세요.**  
The mild one, please.

## 2. A powerful ordering frame

### N 주세요

**물 주세요.**  
Water, please.

**메뉴 주세요.**  
A menu, please.

In real orders, you often add a quantity.

### N 하나 주세요

**떡볶이 하나 주세요.**  
One tteokbokki, please.

**샌드위치 하나 주세요.**  
One sandwich, please.

### N 한 잔 주세요

**커피 한 잔 주세요.**  
One coffee, please.

**차 한 잔 주세요.**  
One tea, please.

### Quantity chunks for this unit

This unit starts with two high-value ordering chunks:

- **하나**
- **한 잔**

Learn them inside the complete order patterns above. The broader Korean counter system will be expanded later only where the relevant official dictionary/teaching sources are attached.

## 3. 을/를 when describing what you eat or drink

Compare:

**고기를 먹어요.**  
I eat meat.

**채소를 먹어요.**  
I eat vegetables.

**커피를 마셔요.**  
I drink coffee.

### Official grammar note — 을/를

The National Institute of Korean Language’s Korean Basic Dictionary defines **을/를** as an object particle marking an object directly affected by an action.

- after a consonant → **을**
  - 밥을
  - 물을
- after a vowel → **를**
  - 커피를
  - 고기를

**Official grammar ref: G_OBJECT_EULREUL**

Compare the unit examples:

**저는 떡볶이를 먹어요.**  
I eat tteokbokki.

**떡볶이 하나 주세요.**  
One tteokbokki, please.

This unit teaches **떡볶이 하나 주세요** as a complete service-request pattern. It does **not** derive a general particle-omission rule from this comparison.

## 4. Change one condition

### Spiciness

**많이 매워요?**  
Is it very spicy?

**조금 매워요.**  
It is a little spicy.

**안 매운 것도 있어요.**  
There is a mild version too.

**안 매운 걸로 주세요.**  
The mild one, please.

### Takeaway

**포장해 주세요.**  
To go, please. / Please pack it to go.

The staff may ask:

**여기서 드시고 가세요, 아니면 포장이세요?**

You do not need to analyze the entire sentence yet.

You only need to understand the choice:

> for here or to go?

and be able to answer:

**포장해 주세요.**

# Living Korean
## Safe learner Korean and real service Korean

A safe, natural default:

**아메리카노 한 잔 주세요.**

A compact order you may hear or use:

**이거 하나 주세요.**

Staff-side language you should recognize:

**뭐 드릴까요?**  
What can I get you?

**따뜻한 걸로 드릴까요, 아이스로 드릴까요?**  
Would you like it hot or iced?

**포장이세요?**  
To go?

### Optional contemporary shorthand

You may see **아아** used for 아이스 아메리카노 in some contexts.

It is useful recognition material, but it is **not required beginner production**.

> A word being used by some Koreans does not automatically make it a core A1 target.

---

## Official grammar note — 주세요

The National Institute of Korean Language’s **Sejong Korean 1 Teacher’s Guide** teaches **-(으)세요** as a form used for a command or request. Its examples include **사과를 주세요**, and its conjugation table shows **주다 → 주세요**.

In this unit, **주세요** is therefore taught as a **request form built from 주다**.

**Official grammar ref: G_SERVICE_JUSEYO**

### Localization note — not a grammar definition

In a café or restaurant, natural English renderings of a sentence such as **아메리카노 한 잔 주세요** can include:

- **Can I get an Americano, please?**
- **Can I have an Americano, please?**
- **I'll have an Americano, please.**

A close compositional gloss such as **“Please give me …”** helps show the relationship to **주다**, while the lines above show common context-appropriate English service wording. These English choices are **translations/localization**, not the grammatical definition of **주세요**.

### Quantity localization note — not a grammar definition

For this unit, connect the Korean chunk:

**커피 한 잔**

with the meaning needed in an order, for example:

**one coffee / one cup of coffee**

The English wording is a translation choice. This page does not use that comparison to define the entire Korean counter system.

## 5. Sound and rhythm

### 주세요

Say it as one smooth phrase.

**주세요**

### 한 잔 주세요

Group it as:

**한 잔 | 주세요**

### 떡볶이 하나 주세요

Group it as:

**떡볶이 | 하나 | 주세요**

Your goal is clear, comfortable speech.

Speed is not the goal.

## 8. Speaking mission

Work with a partner and switch roles.

### Customer card

Include:

1. one menu item;
2. one quantity;
3. one condition.

Possible conditions:

- mild
- iced
- to go

### Staff card

Use two:

- 뭐 드릴까요?
- 많이 매워요. 괜찮으세요?
- 아이스로 드릴까요?
- 포장이세요?

The staff phrases are mainly **interaction input**.

You do not need to memorize all of them as productive grammar.

## 9. Exit task

Close the explanation pages.

Look at a menu and order without translating first.

Success means you can:

- name the item;
- use 하나 or 한 잔;
- use 주세요;
- ask or change one condition;
- respond to one short staff question.

Possible answers:

**아메리카노 한 잔 주세요. 아이스로 주세요.**

**떡볶이 하나 주세요. 많이 매워요?**
