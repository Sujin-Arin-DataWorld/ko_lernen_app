# Korean 1A · Unit 01
# First greetings and asking again

## By the end of this unit, you can:

- Greet someone briefly and respond with yes/no in a first-contact situation.
- Ask politely for one repetition when you did not catch something.

## Why this unit exists

Keep a first interaction going even when you do not understand everything.

## 1. Core scene

**Official**
안녕하세요. 여권 주세요.
*Hello. Your passport, please.*

**Learner**
네, 여기 있어요.
*Yes, here you are.*

**Official**
한국은 처음이세요?
*Is this your first time in Korea?*

**Learner**
죄송해요. 다시 말씀해 주세요.
*Sorry. Could you say that again?*

**Official**
한국이 처음이에요?
*Is this your first time in Korea?*

**Learner**
네, 한국이 처음이에요.
*Yes, this is my first time in Korea.*

### First task

Do not translate every word. Identify:
- who is speaking;
- what the immediate goal is;
- which line solves the problem.

## 2. What you should be able to say

- **안녕하세요**
- **네**
- **아니요**
- **죄송해요**
- **다시 말씀해 주세요**
- **감사합니다**

These are the productive chunks for this unit.

### Productive grammar

- **-아/어 주세요** — polite request

The goal is not to memorize labels. Use the forms to solve the scene.

### Useful formulaic production

- **감사합니다** — use now as a fixed chunk: 감사합니다

Use these chunks now; study the full grammar later.


## 3. Understand first — do not overlearn yet

- **이에요/예요** — appears naturally in the airport response; active copula work begins in Unit 02
- **-(으)시-** — subject honorification appears in authentic service speech; do not teach as generic extra-politeness marker
- **합니다체 어미 전반** — full formal-style morphology is deferred; fixed greetings may still be used as chunks

Authentic input can contain grammar above your current production target. Understanding it does not mean you must produce it now.

## 4. Living Korean

### Pragmatics

- Politeness in service encounters is carried strongly by Korean sentence endings.
- Asking for repetition is a normal interaction strategy, not a failure.
- A short response can be completely appropriate.

### Culture/context

- Korean politeness is not equivalent to simply adding sir/ma'am or using a more formal English word.

Avoid the rule “Koreans always say X.” Learn who says what, to whom, and why.

## 5. English-speaker transfer notes

### EN-A1-SPEECHLEVEL

**Risk:** Expecting politeness to be optional lexical courtesy rather than sentence-final grammar.

**Teaching response:** Treat sentence ending as relationship information. Keep 해요체 productive default; introduce register switching as recognition/social event.

### EN-A1-HONORIFIC-SI

**Risk:** Interpreting subject honorification as a general extra-politeness marker.

**Teaching response:** Separate 'respecting the subject referent' from 'speaking politely to the listener'. Use person-role diagrams, not 'add 시 to be more polite'.

## 6. Sound focus

- 안녕하세요 rhythm
- 네? vs 네. intonation
- 말씀해 주세요 phrase rhythm

## 7. Input mission

- Identify the relationship in a greeting.
- Distinguish statement and question intonation.
- Listen for a repetition request.

## 8. Output mission

- Greet a new person.
- Ask for one repetition without switching to English/German.

## 9. Reading

- Decode 4–6 Hangul Zero carryover words in simple signs/cards.

## 10. Writing

- Write your own name in Hangul where feasible.

## 11. Interaction mission

Do a 30-second first-contact role-play with one intentionally missed utterance.

## 12. Exit task

You must be able to produce:

- 안녕하세요
- 다시 말씀해 주세요
- 감사합니다

You must be able to recognize:

- 네?
- 한국은 처음이세요?

**Success:** Keep the interaction going after one comprehension breakdown.

## Production ceiling

Short single-clause A1 turns; do not teach the full honorific system here.
