# Korean 1A · Unit 02
# Name and where you're from

## By the end of this unit, you can:

- Say your name and where you are from, and ask the other person simple identity questions.
- Continue a short introduction without repeating an explicit subject in every Korean sentence.

## Why this unit exists

Build a real short exchange instead of reciting a memorized self-introduction paragraph.

## 1. Core scene

**Sujin**
안녕하세요. 저는 수진이에요.
*Hi. I'm Sujin.*

**Learner**
안녕하세요. 저는 크리스티안이에요.
*Hi. I'm Christian.*

**Sujin**
어디에서 오셨어요?
*Where are you from?*

**Learner**
독일에서 왔어요.
*I'm from Germany.*

**Learner**
죄송해요. 이름을 다시 말해 주세요.
*Sorry, could you say your name again, please?*

**Sujin**
네, 수진이에요. 수진.
*Yes, Sujin. Su-jin.*

### First task

Do not translate every word. Identify:
- who is speaking;
- what the immediate goal is;
- which line solves the problem.

## 2. What you should be able to say

- **저는 ___이에요/예요**
- **어디에서 왔어요?**
- **___에서 왔어요**
- **이름을 다시 말해 주세요**
- **저도 그래요/저도 좋아해요**

These are the productive chunks for this unit.

### Productive grammar

- **은/는** — set personal topic
- **이에요/예요** — identify self
- **N에서 왔어요** — origin/source

The goal is not to memorize labels. Use the forms to solve the scene.



## 3. Understand first — do not overlearn yet

- **-(으)셨어요** — authentic polite question form may appear; productive honorific morphology is not analyzed exhaustively here

Authentic input can contain grammar above your current production target. Understanding it does not mean you must produce it now.

## 4. Living Korean

### Pragmatics

- One self-disclosure plus one follow-up often feels more natural than a rapid series of questions.
- Identity questions such as job, age, and residence are optional and context-sensitive.

### Culture/context

- First meetings can involve relationship-positioning questions, but they should not be taught as a mandatory checklist.

Avoid the rule “Koreans always say X.” Learn who says what, to whom, and why.

## 5. English-speaker transfer notes

### EN-A1-TOPIC-SUBJECT

**Risk:** Treating both as a single English-style subject marker or swapping them without discourse motivation.

**Teaching response:** Teach mini-context pairs: '저는...' for frame/topic vs '누가? 제가...' for focused/new subject. Delay exhaustive theory.

### EN-A1-SUBJECT-OMISSION

**Risk:** Repeating 저는/제가 or explicit pronouns because English clauses normally require overt subjects.

**Teaching response:** Show dialogue pairs where the first subject establishes context and subsequent Korean clauses omit it naturally.

### EN-A1-WORDORDER

**Risk:** Transferring English SVO sequencing into Korean production.

**Teaching response:** Use movable noun-card chunks but keep predicate as the final anchor. Avoid metalanguage-heavy SOV lectures on day one.

## 6. Sound focus

- 이에요/예요 linking
- country/name syllable blocks
- 왔어요 pronunciation awareness

## 7. Input mission

- Identify name and origin in two short introductions.
- Notice when Korean drops a subject after the topic is clear.

## 8. Output mission

- Give a two-turn self-introduction.
- Ask one natural follow-up and return the question.

## 9. Reading

- Read Korean personal names and country names in Hangul.

## 10. Writing

- Write a two-line profile card.

## 11. Interaction mission

Do a 45-second speed-meeting and discover one point in common.

## 12. Exit task

You must be able to produce:

- 저는 ___이에요/예요
- ___에서 왔어요

You must be able to recognize:

- 어디에서 오셨어요?

**Success:** Exchange basic identity information without reciting a memorized paragraph.

## Production ceiling

One idea per sentence; no résumé-style extended self-introduction.

This unit is part of a spaced-recycling curriculum. The target language returns in later contexts.
