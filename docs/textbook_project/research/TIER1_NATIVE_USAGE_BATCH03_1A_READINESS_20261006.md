## 1. First meetings are not miniature résumés

### Korean
Useful stable A1 forms:
- 안녕하세요. 저는 수진이에요.
- 저는 독일에서 왔어요.
- 지금 프랑크푸르트에 살아요.
- 무슨 일 하세요?
- 어디 사세요?
- 취미가 뭐예요?
- 저도 그거 좋아해요.

Real-life observation:
first meetings often proceed as short question/response chains. Personal essays and contemporary advice also warn that firing many questions in sequence can feel like an interview.

Publishing implication:
teach **one self-disclosure + one follow-up + one return question**, not a memorized paragraph.

### English
Stable first-meeting forms:
- Hi, I'm ...
- Nice to meet you.
- Where are you from?
- What do you do?
- What do you like to do for fun?
- You can call me ...

British Council A1 material confirms short introductions and social follow-up rather than long prepared monologues.

### German
Stable A1:
- Hallo, ich bin ...
- Ich heiße ...
- Ich komme aus ...
- Ich wohne in ...
- Ich arbeite als ...
- Freut mich.
- Und du?

Goethe A1 explicitly includes introducing oneself and asking personal information. Community evidence is then used to decide when du/Sie is socially appropriate.

## 2. Contact exchange is a boundary act

### Korean
Natural contemporary communication often names the channel:
- 카톡 하세요?
- 제가 카톡 보낼게요.
- 나중에 카톡해요.
- 도착하면 바로 연락할게요.

KakaoTalk also functions lexically as:
- app name;
- message;
- verb-like 카톡하다.

### English
- Can I get your number?
- I'll give you my number.
- Text me when you get there.
- I'll text you later.
- Can we reschedule?
- Does another day work for you?

Important:
asking for a number is not a neutral mechanical step. Low-pressure alternatives matter.

### German
- Wollen wir Nummern austauschen?
- Sollen wir auf WhatsApp weiterschreiben?
- Ich kann dir meine Nummer geben.
- Schreib mir einfach.
- Ich melde mich später.
- Können wir den Termin verschieben?

German casual contact strongly favors schreiben / sich melden / Bescheid geben rather than formal kontaktieren.

## 3. Communication repair must distinguish three failures

The textbook now separates:

1. **I did not hear it.**
2. **I heard it but do not know the meaning.**
3. **I think I understood and want confirmation.**

### Korean
Hearing:
- 잘 못 들었어요.
- 다시 말씀해 주세요.
- 천천히 말씀해 주세요.

Meaning:
- 무슨 뜻이에요?
- 이 단어가 무슨 뜻이에요?

Confirmation:
- 제가 잘 이해한 게 맞아요?
- 그러니까 ○○라는 말이에요?

### English
Hearing:
- Sorry?
- I didn't catch that.
- Could you say that again?

Meaning:
- What does that mean?

Confirmation:
- Do you mean ...?

### German
Hearing:
- Wie bitte?
- Noch mal, bitte.
- Könnten Sie das bitte wiederholen?

Meaning:
- Was bedeutet das?
- Was heißt das?

Confirmation:
- Meinst du ...?

This split becomes a core A1 learner strategy, not a side vocabulary box.

## 4. Politeness is not one shared switch

### Korean
The relationship is continuously encoded through sentence endings and address choices.
Key social actions:
- 우리 말 놓을까요?
- 반말해도 괜찮아요?
- 저는 존댓말이 더 편해요.
- 어떻게 불러 드리면 될까요?

### English
English normally does not have an equivalent grammatical speech-level switch.
Many speakers move quickly to first names.
Sir/ma'am vary strongly by region, generation and institution.

### German
German does have an explicit address choice:
- du
- Sie

But it is still **not equivalent** to Korean 반말/존댓말.
Community evidence confirms explicit negotiation:
- Wollen wir uns duzen?
- Wir können uns gern duzen.

## Cross-language rule

Never store:
`polite Korean = formal English = German Sie`

Store:
- relationship
- setting
- address preference
- speech style
- communicative function
- language-specific realization
