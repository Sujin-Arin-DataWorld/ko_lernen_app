### Critical English-speaker teaching decision

Do **not** explain:
> -(으)시- = “more polite”

Explain two independent questions:
1. **Who is the subject/referent?**
2. **How am I speaking to the listener?**

This is especially important because recent multi-method research found English-speaking learners often interpreted subject honorification as general politeness rather than its specific referential function.

### Particle teaching decision

English-L1 particle errors persist across levels. Therefore:

`Unit 2 topic/subject`
does **not** close the topic.

Particles recycle through:
- identity
- shopping
- ordering
- location
- transport
- contact
- repair

with different information structures.

## German-L1 priorities

| Priority | Target | Risk | Tier | 1A response |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | 에/에서/(으)로 | direct German preposition/case mapping | A | semantic-space map |
| 2 | Korean particles vs German case | 조사 = Kasusartikel assumption | B | explicit “조사 ≠ Kasusartikel” box |
| 3 | 은/는 vs 이/가 | search for German case equivalent | B | information structure, not case labels |
| 4 | 존댓말/반말 vs du/Sie | false one-to-one mapping | B | separate social maps |
| 5 | -(으)시- | search for German honorific pronoun equivalent | B | subject-referent diagram |
| 6 | word order | “Korean = German subordinate clause” overgeneralization | B | analogy only, not equivalence |
| 7 | subject omission | restore ich/du in Korean | C | contextual omission examples |
| 8 | counters | German numeral+noun transfer | C | task chunks |

### Critical German-speaker teaching decision

Never teach:
> 존댓말 = Sie  
> 반말 = du

A German translation can naturally use **du** while Korean speakers still use **해요체**, depending on relationship and stage of familiarity.

Likewise, Korean subject honorification has no direct German grammatical counterpart.

### 에/에서/(으)로

For German learners, prefer a **conceptual spatial map**:

- destination / target → 에
- current existence/location → 에
- location of an action → 에서
- path / means / direction → (으)로

Then attach German translations afterward.

Do not begin with:
- 에 = in / an / zu
- 에서 = in / bei
- (으)로 = mit / nach

because the mapping collapses across contexts.

# Pilot instrumentation

For future adult learner pilot, record:
- error type
- target form
- learner L1
- whether error followed explicit explanation or free production
- self-correction after prompt
- recurrence after delayed review

Do not record only right/wrong.

The purpose is to discover whether our explanations actually reduce predictable transfer errors.
