# VOCABULARY SYLLABUS — durable core + living language

## Architecture
Vocabulary is split into four layers:
1. CORE — durable high-utility vocabulary required for level outcomes.
2. SITUATIONAL — domain words needed for a specific unit/task.
3. PRAGMATIC — discourse markers, reactions, address terms, softeners and interactional chunks.
4. TREND — dated slang/memes/current expressions; never required as stable core knowledge.

## Every lexical record stores
lemma; sense_id; level; frequency_band; topic; function; register; age/social context; region; spoken_written; collocations; morphology; counters/classifiers; example_ko; paraphrase_ko; translation_en; translation_de; concept_notes_en; concept_notes_de; false_friend_notes; currency_status; first_seen/last_verified; source_ids; review_status.

## Coverage strategy
Use the official 1–6 curriculum vocabulary inventories as a coverage benchmark, then prioritize by communicative utility, contemporary corpus evidence and unit needs.
Existing Hangul Sori seed: 558 KR/DE/EN vocabulary entries, strongest in A1–B2 daily-life domains. These must be deduplicated by sense, re-leveled where necessary and enriched with register/collocation/pragmatics.

## Level 1 semantic domains
identity; people/relationships; numbers/time; food/drink; home; transport; places; daily actions; weather; basic feelings; shopping; devices; café/convenience store; essential digital actions.
High-value chunks count as lexical items when learned holistically: 괜찮아요, 잘 모르겠어요, 잠시만요, 여기요, 맞아요, 진짜요?, 어떻게 가요?, ~주세요.

## Level 2 domains
appointments; travel; delivery; online shopping; health basics; hobbies; streaming/music; study/work routines; messaging; restaurants; banking; public services; describing experiences and preferences.

## Level 3 domains
career/job search; apartment/housing; workplace collaboration; reviews/recommendations; relationships/dating; customer service; social media; personal finance basics; troubleshooting; opinions and storytelling.

## Level 4 domains
AI/digital tools; privacy; sustainability; cost of living; workplace culture; news/media; consumer rights; social issues; nuanced emotions; disagreement; negotiation; public services and bureaucracy.

## Levels 5–6 domains
economics; technology policy; climate; demographics; housing systems; education; labor; ethics; media literacy; culture criticism; research/academic discourse; policy and public debate.
Prioritize productive academic collocations and discourse bundles, not rare dictionary words for their own sake.

## Translation policy
Never force one Korean word into one EN/DE gloss. Store senses separately.
Example: 아쉽다 needs situation-specific mappings such as “that's a shame / I wish…” and “schade / bedauerlich / ich finde es schade,” with Korean usage frames.
Conversely, English/German polysemy must remain visible so the database can later reverse direction for Korean learners of EN/DE.

## Trend Capsule policy
Candidate examples may include dated youth/internet forms such as Korean 갓생/중꺾마/느좋/추구미/럭키비키, English brain rot/rizz/rage bait, and German Aura/das crazy/Crashout.
None is publishable merely because it appeared online. Each requires: attested source, date, community/region, meaning-in-context, register warning, current-status check and native review.
Trend pages should teach “what this sounds like and who might say it,” not encourage indiscriminate imitation.

## Recycling target
A new lexical item should recur across multiple modes: first encounter → comprehension → collocation → production → delayed retrieval → new context. Frequency in the book must be tracked programmatically, not by intuition.
