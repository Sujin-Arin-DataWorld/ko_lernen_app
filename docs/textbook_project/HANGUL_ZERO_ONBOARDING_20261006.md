# Hangul Zero — Adult Onboarding Contract

Date: 2026-10-06

## Purpose

A beginner adult should experience:

> “I can decode Korean.”

before experiencing:

> “I have to memorize an unfamiliar alphabet.”

Hangul Zero is therefore not a 가나다 chart chapter. It is a short **discovery → assembly → decoding → sound-awareness** sequence.

## Research basis

A 2025 experimental study comparing two Hangeul teaching orders found higher test and learner-perception means for a group taught through a sequence based on Hunminjeongeum creation principles; the group difference was statistically significant (p < .001).

A 2024 study of beginner English-speaking Korean learners using romanization argues for realistic, limited use of romanization together with:
- recognition of pronunciation variation;
- daily-conversation content;
- listening/practice;
- explicit transition to Hangeul-centered notation.

## Design principles

1. **Pattern before inventory**
   - learners see families and construction logic before full charts.

2. **Read real Korean immediately**
   - 이름, 카페, 버스, 서울, 한국, 안녕-like meaningful material as soon as decodable.

3. **Block assembly is visible**
   - consonant + vowel → syllable block
   - left-right vs top-bottom layout.

4. **Sound != Roman letter**
   - Latin letters may be a temporary orientation aid, never the sound authority.

5. **Romanization sunset**
   - no line-by-line romanization in the main 1A book.
   - optional first-session support only.
   - Korean script becomes the default quickly.

6. **Adults are not children**
   - explain the design logic and articulation.
   - avoid cartoon alphabet drilling as the primary method.

7. **Recognition → retrieval**
   - learners must rebuild and read, not only match shapes.

8. **Sound contrasts need ears, eyes and mouth**
   - plain/aspirated/tense contrast introduced through perception and articulation, not English spelling analogies.

## Proposed 60–90 minute onboarding

### H0-1 — The block idea

Goal:
understand that 한 is not three letters in a row but one syllable block assembled from sounds.

Show:
- ㅎ + ㅏ + ㄴ → 한
- ㄱ + ㅜ + ㄱ → 국

Learner action:
drag/arrange jamo into blocks.

No grammar.

### H0-2 — Vowel architecture

Start from visual/articulatory families rather than 가나다 order.

Working sequence inspired by the 2025 proposal:
- ㅡ, ㅣ
- ㅗ, ㅜ
- ㅏ, ㅓ
- ㅐ, ㅔ

Then:
- ㅛ, ㅠ, ㅑ, ㅕ
- compound vowels later, as needed.

Adult explanation:
the system is constructed, not arbitrary.

### H0-3 — Consonant architecture

Early core:
- ㅇ, ㄱ, ㄴ, ㄷ, ㄹ, ㅅ, ㅈ, ㅁ, ㅂ

Then build families:
- aspiration: ㅋ, ㅌ, ㅊ, ㅍ, ㅎ
- tense: ㄲ, ㄸ, ㅆ, ㅉ, ㅃ

Do not tell English/German learners:
- ㄱ = g
- ㅋ = k
- ㄲ = kk

as if those are exact phonemes.

Instead:
contrast the Korean categories through sound and airflow/tension.

### H0-4 — Decode your first real world

Mini signs/menu:
- 카페
- 버스
- 택시
- 한국
- 서울
- 김밥
- 라면

Task:
read before translating.

Reward:
“I decoded it.”

### H0-5 — Batchim as closing the block

Introduce only a small useful set first:
- ㄴ
- ㅁ
- ㄹ
- ㅇ
- ㄱ

Examples:
- 한
- 밥
- 집
- 문
- 물

Teach:
written final consonant and actual sound are related but pronunciation rules will expand gradually.

### H0-6 — Your first name / contact

Learner:
- recognizes own name if represented in Hangeul;
- reads a few classmate/persona names;
- types simple Korean on device.

This immediately connects Hangeul to identity and digital life.

### H0-7 — First repair phrases

Decode:
- 네
- 아니요
- 다시
- 천천히

Then hear:
- 네?
- 다시 말씀해 주세요.
- 천천히 말씀해 주세요.

This bridges Hangul Zero directly into 1A-01 and 1A-08.

### H0-8 — Romanization graduation

Final onboarding task:
read 8–12 known items **without romanization**.

If learner needs support:
audio + Hangeul + articulatory cue, not permanent Latin transcription.

## EN-specific support

Likely early pronunciation risks:
- ㅡ and ㅢ
- plain/aspirated/tense consonant categories
- treating romanized g/k, d/t etc. as English phonemes.

EN notes should say:
> The Latin spelling is a navigation aid, not the Korean sound.

## DE-specific support

Do not let familiar Latin spelling imply German pronunciation values.

Examples:
- romanized j is especially risky because German <j> strongly cues /j/.
- eo/eu are not German letter sequences to pronounce compositionally.

Therefore the DE edition should rely even less on intuitive Roman-letter pronunciation and more on:
- audio
- mouth/articulation diagrams
- Hangeul itself.

## Success criteria

A learner passes Hangul Zero when they can:
- explain the syllable-block principle;
- assemble CV/CVC blocks;
- read a small set of new decodable words;
- distinguish several basic vowel/consonant contrasts by ear;
- type/read basic Korean without line-by-line romanization;
- use one repair phrase when they cannot understand.

## What Hangul Zero deliberately does not finish

It does not attempt:
- all batchim rules;
- all liaison/assimilation;
- complete phonology;
- perfect native pronunciation;
- exhaustive compound vowels.

Those recur throughout 1A–2A.

## Publishing format

Student book:
- 8–12 compact pages.

Workbook:
- block assembly + decoding + dictation.

App:
- interactive jamo block assembly
- audio discrimination
- typing
- sign/menu decoding.

Teacher guide:
- EN/DE pronunciation traps
- romanization sunset rule
- optional slower pathway.

Audio:
- human-reviewed spoken surface only.
