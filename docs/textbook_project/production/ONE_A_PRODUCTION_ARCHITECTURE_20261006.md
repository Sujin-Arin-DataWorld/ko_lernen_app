## Exercise-density control

A unit should not grow simply because more app items are available.

### Student book target
- about 10 activity blocks
- at least 3 open-production blocks
- at least 1 interaction block
- at least 1 delayed-retrieval block
- closed-format activities: max ~40%
- productive activities: min ~35%
- interaction + retrieval: min ~25%

### Workbook target
- about 9 activity blocks
- 24–32 micro-items
- max 2 pure translation items
- at least 1 free-production block
- at least 1 delayed-retrieval block
- 1 L1-transfer diagnostic block

## Why this matters

The existing app source pool is large.

That is useful for:
- recycling;
- item variation;
- adaptive practice;
- later digital expansion.

It is **not** a reason to print every exercise.

Print should preserve:
- conceptual progression;
- manageable density;
- visible page rhythm;
- enough white space for adult learners;
- deliberate transition from input to production.

## Final review

The final review should deliberately remove unit labels.

Learners perform:
- multi-scene interaction;
- listen-and-repair;
- short reading;
- short message writing;
- cross-unit retrieval.

The goal is transfer, not recognition of a familiar chapter.

## Edition policy

EN and DE editions share:
- Korean semantic target;
- unit architecture;
- content IDs;
- assessment intent.

They do **not** share:
- learner-facing explanation wording;
- transfer-risk explanations;
- translation choices;
- some cultural comparison notes.

## Hard production rules

1. No line-by-line romanization after Hangul Zero.
2. Authentic input may exceed the production ceiling only when the item is explicitly formulaic or recognition-only.
3. Every unit has one real interaction task.
4. Every unit has delayed retrieval.
5. EN and DE explanations are authored independently.
6. TTS remains Jin-owned.
7. Human approval remains a separate gate from model editorial work.
