# HANGUL SORI — Visual Style Guide

**Style Name:** Faceted Minhwa (모던 면 분할 민화)
**Project:** Hangul Sori (한글소리) — Korean learning app for German-speaking learners
**Version:** 1.0
**Last Updated:** 2026-05-21

---

## TL;DR

A modern editorial illustration language that fuses **mid-century geometric reduction** (Saul Bass, Charley Harper) with **Korean minhwa folk painting iconography** (까치호랑이, 일월오봉도, 단청). Every subject is built from clean flat color planes — no outlines, no smooth gradients within shapes — with volume emerging through hard-edged value shifts between facets. The whole image carries a subtle hanji paper grain texture overlay for warm tactile feel.

The style sits at the intersection of three traditions:
- **Korean minhwa** folk painting (iconography, symbolism, color)
- **Mid-century modernist** illustration (geometric reduction, flat planes)
- **Contemporary editorial design** (premium magazine-cover quality)

If you reduce this style to one sentence: *"Joseon folk painting iconography rendered in mid-century geometric facets on aged hanji paper."*

---

## CORE PRINCIPLES (Non-negotiable)

### 1. Faceted Geometric Construction
Every subject — tiger, magpie, mountain, hanok roof, plum branch — is constructed from clean angular flat color planes. Shapes feel like cut paper or stained glass. NOT smooth, NOT all organic curves — angular and confident.

### 2. NO Outlines on Subjects
Pure color planes only. NO black outlines, NO defining strokes around objects. Shapes are defined by their fill colors meeting adjacent shapes.

### 3. NO Smooth Gradients Within Shapes
Each facet is a flat color block. Volume and form emerge through hard-edged value shifts between adjacent facets (lighter plane vs shadow plane).

**ONE EXCEPTION:** A single intentional soft gradient is permitted per image, typically:
- Sky atmosphere (cream → celadon, or navy → deeper navy)
- A single warm glow halo (lantern, sunset)

Everything else uses hard-edged facet transitions.

### 4. Hanji Paper Grain Texture
A subtle paper grain texture overlay is applied across the entire image, with optional faint aged spots — like an aged Korean mulberry paper print. Without this, the style feels too sterile/vector-flat.

### 5. Restricted Palette
Saturated but slightly muted jewel tones, NOT candy colors. The palette is intentional and limited — every asset draws from the same color family below.

### 6. Authentic Korean Iconography
Korean cultural symbols are used with intention, not decoration:
- Korean tiger with 王 forehead character
- Magpie with gat (갓) hat
- Dancheong color bands
- Curved hanok eaves with upturned horns
- Sagunja motifs (plum, orchid, chrysanthemum, bamboo) — seasonal
- Sun (vermillion) + moon (muted indigo)

## ICONOGRAPHY VOCABULARY

The visual library we draw from. Use these motifs with intention.

### Animals (use sparingly, never random)

- **Korean Tiger (호랑이)** — Always shows 王 character on forehead. Confident frontal pose with slight 3/4 turn. Sharp almond-shaped amber-gold eyes. Angular charcoal stripes. Dignified guardian energy. **NEVER cute/chibi.**

- **Magpie with Gat (갓 까치)** — Black + white body, gold-amber beak, small tilted gat hat (flat oval brim + tall cylindrical crown + thin gold band). Small, perches on tiger shoulder / hanok eave / pine branch.

- **Crane (학)** — Use VERY sparingly. Only when scenery directly calls for it (standing on mountain peak in folding screen panel). Past attempts have felt random — default to omitting.

### Architectural Elements
- **Curved Tile Roof (기와지붕)** — Strong upturned eave horns (처마끝), individual semicircle tile facets along top, dark slate-charcoal primary
- **Dancheong Band (단청)** — Teal base with alternating geometric color squares (red/gold/cream); small flower or lotus motifs inside squares
- **Wooden Columns (기둥)** — Warm walnut vertical rectangles with darker shadow facet on inner edge
- **Rafter Ends (서까래)** — Row of small uniform brown rectangles along eave drip line, like dark teeth following the curve
- **Roof Tile Cap (망와)** — Tiny chrysanthemum disc at eave tip in cream + ochre
- **Lattice Paper Door (창호지문)** — Grid of small rectangular panes in walnut frames, with cream paper backing
- **Folding Screen (병풍)** — Multi-panel (4-6 panels) with minhwa scenes within each panel, framed by thin walnut

### Nature Motifs — Seasonal Markers

| Season | Motif | Color Style |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| Spring | Plum Blossom (매화) | 5-petal pale pink + center gold dot, dark walnut angular branch |
| Summer | Bamboo (대나무) | Vertical green stalks with segment lines, angular leaf clusters |
| Summer | Lotus (연꽃 + 연잎) | Layered pink petal flowers + round sage pad leaves |
| Autumn | Maple Leaf (단풍잎) | 5-lobed deep vermillion or ochre gold with visible vein facets |
| Winter | Persimmon (감/곶감) | Hanging orange disc fruits on dark cord, dark stem cap |
| Timeless | Pine Tree (소나무) | Angular dark green triangular needle clusters on warm walnut trunk |
| Timeless | Mountain Peaks (산봉우리) | Irworobongdo style, overlapping flat silhouettes in receding greens |

### Celestial Elements
- **Vermillion Sun (해)** — Bright red-orange disc (`#A8332E` to `#C24A45`), often upper-right, partially hidden behind a peak
- **Muted Indigo Crescent Moon (달)** — Small flat shape (`#1F2E5C`), upper area
- **Pale Ivory Full Moon** — Dark mode variant (`#F4E8D0` at ~30% opacity)
- **Cloud Scrolls (구름문양)** — Flat geometric curl shapes in ivory cream
- **Stars** — Tiny dot facets, scattered subtly (dark mode only)

### Cultural Objects
- **Hanji Book (책)** — Slim bound, cloth tie binding visible as thin spine stripe, palette colors
- **Calligraphy Brush (붓)** — Cherry-wood handle + gold collar + sharp triangular black bristle
- **Inkstone (벼루)** — Flat rectangular slate, circular ink pool depression with glossy facet
- **Ink Stick (먹)** — Long hexagonal black with small gold cap
- **Celadon Teacup/Bowl (청자)** — Teal-green with thin saucer, darker shadow facet for volume
- **Folding Fan (부채)** — Paper fan with bamboo ribs fanning outward, tiny dancheong accent
- **Korean Knot (매듭)** — Colorful cord work tassel decoration
- **Wind Chime (풍경)** — Bronze bell + fish-shaped pendant + tassel
- **Ink Seal (인장)** — Small square red stamp with cream character inside (often 王)
- **Hanji Lantern (한지등)** — Rectangular, grid frame structure, warm gold glow from within, red tassel

## COMPOSITION PRINCIPLES

### 1. Cinematic Depth Through Overlapping Planes
Even in flat compositions, depth emerges from layered front-to-back planes:
1. Foreground subjects
2. Mid-ground architecture or objects
3. Background landscape
4. Sky/atmosphere

NEVER use perspective tricks — depth comes purely from overlapping shapes and atmospheric value shifts (closer = warmer/darker, farther = cooler/paler).

### 2. Generous Negative Space
Breathing room is critical. Foreground objects typically occupy <30% of canvas. Sky, landscape, or empty hanji surfaces dominate. The image should feel **open**, not packed.

### 3. Balanced Warm/Cool Anchors
Every composition needs both:
- **Warm anchors**: walnut wood, sun, tiger coat, dancheong jewel tones
- **Cool anchors**: slate roofs, sage mountains, celadon, pale sky

Pure warm = sepia death (누리끼리). Pure cool = sterile.

### 4. Tonal Range
Light cream highlights → mid-tone landscape → deep charcoal silhouette anchors. The image must have proper tonal range, not uniform mid-gray wash.

### 5. Clustered Accents, Not Scattered
Dancheong color dots and small motifs are clustered in **2 loose groupings**, NOT scattered randomly across the whole canvas. Random scattering looks AI-generated and lacks intention.

### 6. One Soft Gradient Maximum
Per image, only ONE soft gradient is allowed — usually sky atmosphere or a single light halo. Everything else uses hard-edged facet transitions.

### 7. Silhouette Readability
Subjects must read clearly at thumbnail size. Test: would the tiger still be recognizable at 100px? If silhouettes blur into background, increase tonal contrast.

## STYLE DISCIPLINE — DOs & DON'Ts

### DO ✓
- ✓ Use authentic Korean minhwa iconography
- ✓ Build subjects from angular geometric facets
- ✓ Apply subtle hanji paper grain texture overlay
- ✓ Cluster decorative accents intentionally (in 2 loose groups)
- ✓ Maintain warm/cool color balance
- ✓ Leave generous breathing room
- ✓ Use restricted palette hex values
- ✓ Include the 王 character on tiger forehead
- ✓ Use gat hat for any magpie figure
- ✓ Match seasonal motifs to one consistent season per asset

### DON'T ✗
- ✗ Add outlines around subjects
- ✗ Use smooth gradients within shapes (except one sky gradient)
- ✗ Make tigers cute/chibi (must be dignified guardian)
- ✗ Mix seasonal motifs (no autumn maple + spring plum in same piece)
- ✗ Place animals randomly without compositional reason
- ✗ Use Western seasonal imagery (Christmas decorations, etc.)
- ✗ Apply sepia/monochromatic brown wash
- ✗ Apply uniform dark wash (must have tonal range)
- ✗ Pack composition with objects (breathing room essential)
- ✗ Use realistic/3D/anime/chibi/watercolor styles
- ✗ Render pure vector flat (too sterile — needs grain texture)
- ✗ Add bright candy/neon colors
- ✗ Use cool gray-blue for hanok silhouettes (use neutral charcoal)
- ✗ Render crane without specific compositional reason
- ✗ Mix calligraphy brushwork and modern typography in same set

## COMMON PITFALLS & CORRECTIONS

### "Too sepia / 누리끼리해"
**Symptom:** Image dominated by warm browns, looks like aged photograph
**Cause:** Over-correcting for "warm tone" without cool anchors
**Fix:** Add NEUTRAL CHARCOAL (`#3E3A38`) anchor for hanok silhouettes. Add COOL NEUTRAL GRAY (`#9A938C`) for distant mountains. Explicitly state: *"WARM-DOMINANT with NEUTRAL DARK ANCHORS for tonal contrast. AVOID sepia wash."*

### "Too cold / 차가워"
**Symptom:** Image feels icy, sterile, monochromatic blue-gray
**Cause:** Cool tones unchecked, no warm anchors
**Fix:** Reintroduce warm walnut wood tones, warm sun/lamp glow, dancheong red/gold accents. The image needs both temperature poles.

### "Too closed in / 답답해"
**Symptom:** Image feels packed, walled-in, claustrophobic
**Cause:** Too many framing elements (walls, doors, screens), too many objects
**Fix:** Remove walls/doors/screens or make them peripheral. Increase negative space — sky, landscape, water should dominate. Limit foreground objects to 2-4 max, clustered intentionally.

### "Random animal / 뜬금없는 동물"
**Symptom:** A bird/crane appears without narrative reason
**Cause:** Defaulting to nature motifs without compositional purpose
**Fix:** Either remove the animal entirely, OR give it a clear narrative reason (magpie perches on a specific eave, tiger guards a specific scene). Most assets should have NO animals or just the iconic gat-magpie.

### "Too AI-flat / 너무 평평해"
**Symptom:** Pure vector flat, lifeless, sterile
**Cause:** Missing paper grain texture overlay
**Fix:** Emphasize *"subtle hanji paper grain texture overlay across the entire image"* in prompt. Reference traditional Korean mulberry paper printmaking.

### "Mixed seasons / 계절 섞임"
**Symptom:** Autumn maple leaves alongside spring plum blossoms in same image
**Cause:** AI hedging by adding multiple seasonal motifs
**Fix:** Commit to ONE season explicitly. State *"Commit to ONE season only: [season]. NO mixing of [other season] motifs."*

### "Building invisible / 건물이 안 보여"
**Symptom:** A hanok scene shows only objects, not the architecture
**Cause:** Going too minimalist on framing
**Fix:** Explicitly require *"the hanok roof and columns MUST be clearly visible — this is essential."* Specify roof + columns + porch as structural framing.

### "Typography mismatch / 글씨체 통일 안 됨"
**Symptom:** Hangul text rendered as calligraphy in one asset, sans-serif in another
**Cause:** Inconsistent typography spec across prompts
**Fix:** Decide on ONE typography style for Hangul text across all assets — **modern Pretendard / Noto Sans KR Bold** recommended for consistency with mobile UI. Reserve calligraphy brushstrokes for decorative seal stamps only.

### "Crane looks random / 학이 뜬금없어"
**Symptom:** A crane stands somewhere with no narrative justification
**Cause:** AI default — crane is a "Korean motif" so it gets added
**Fix:** Explicitly state *"NO crane"* in the prompt unless the composition specifically calls for one (e.g. a folding screen panel of mountains).

## WORKFLOW TIPS

### Generating New Assets
1. **Start with the anchor asset** (`welcome-hero` or `gate`) and lock in the tone first
2. **Always attach 1-2 existing assets** when generating new ones — text prompt alone isn't enough. The reference image carries the style anchor
3. **Generate 3-5 variations** and select the best — first attempt is rarely the final
4. **Add the "match attached reference" closing line** at the end of every prompt

### Iteration Strategy
1. **First pass**: get composition + major elements right
2. **Second pass**: fix color balance (warm/cool, sepia/icy)
3. **Third pass**: tune specific elements (positions, opacity, density)
4. **Final pass**: polish details (typography, decorative accents)

### Brief Triage
When something feels "off," diagnose in this order:
1. **Color temperature** — sepia? icy? unbalanced?
2. **Composition** — too closed? too busy? wrong focal point?
3. **Style** — outlines present? gradients within shapes? missing grain?
4. **Iconography** — random elements? wrong season? cultural accuracy?
